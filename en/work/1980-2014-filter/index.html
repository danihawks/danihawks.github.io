This book investigates the building envelope not as a neutral boundary, but as a filter that mediates its context and produces an archetypal sense of protection. Developed from architectural design courses at the Politecnico di Torino, it follows two connected trajectories. Side A traces the historical construction of the architectural surface through Laugier, Semper and Viollet-le-Duc, then focuses on the 1980 and 2014 Venice Biennales. Side B, Filter, turns toward personal reflection, intertwining theoretical, historical and literary references to define the façade as a protective and operative filter.

Image 1 / 2

Façade

The first chapter begins with the origin myth that emerged during positivism and follows the positions of Laugier and Viollet-le-Duc. It then focuses on Gottfried Semper, whose ideas find a partial pragmatic realization in the avant-garde works of Modernism, where the envelope progressively dematerializes into a floating membrane.

Image 1 / 3

1980

The second chapter reconstructs the context of the first Venice Architecture Biennale. After examining the cultural and semiotic factors behind the collapse of Modernism, it turns to Charles Jencks’s theories of radical eclecticism and to a critical comparison between two emblematic façades from the Strada Novissima: those of OMA and Ricardo Bofill.

Image 1 / 2

2014

The third chapter focuses on the fourteenth Biennale of 2014 and Alejandro Zaera-Polo’s encyclopedic research into the façade. This historiographical mapping places archaic cases beside contemporary expressions, showing how the envelope has become detached from the project’s internal logics and inscribed directly within the economic tensions of the global market.

Image 1 / 2

Filter

The final chapter shifts from historical narration toward theory. It investigates the origins of architecture to identify the primitive archetype of the protection that the work should provide. The research develops a five-stage understanding of the façade as a filter, connecting etymological inquiries to Gottfried Semper’s theory, and closes with three built case studies selected to describe the pragmatic effectiveness of the “façade-filter”.
